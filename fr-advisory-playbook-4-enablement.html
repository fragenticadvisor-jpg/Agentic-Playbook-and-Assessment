Chapter 01
The selling shift: from coverage to value
Seat-based selling is a coverage conversation: how many users, how many sites, how many licences. Consumption selling is a value conversation: how much work do your agents do, and what is that work worth? The commercial motion is fundamentally different — and most GTM teams aren't trained for it.
The shift isn't just in the pitch. It runs through the entire revenue motion — how reps discover, how they qualify, how CS defines health, how expansion gets triggered, and how commission plans incentivise behaviour. A well-designed hybrid pricing model deployed into an unreformed GTM motion will underperform. The commercial model and the GTM motion have to move together.

"The companies that struggle with consumption pricing aren't failing at the model design. They're failing at the GTM transformation. Pricing changes without enablement changes don't land."

The enablement gap is a revenue risk
Most SaaS businesses launch their hybrid model with a product update and a pricing page change. They tell the sales team in an all-hands. They update the deck. But the discovery questions stay the same. The CS health metrics stay the same. The comp plan stays the same. Six months later, the model has launched but the GTM motion hasn't changed — and the consumption revenue never materialises.

The discovery framework for consumption selling
Chapter 02
Discovery: the questions that unlock value conversations
Consumption selling starts in discovery. The questions you ask in the first two meetings determine whether the deal gets qualified on value or on headcount. Here's the framework by deal stage.
Workflow mapping
Displacement sizing
Value quantification
Spend forecasting
Use these questions to map which workflows in the customer's business are candidates for agent automation — before any pricing conversation begins.
"Walk me through a typical day for the team using this product. What tasks are they doing manually that feel repetitive?"
Opens the workflow mapping without framing it as a cost conversation. Customers self-identify displacement candidates.
"Which of those tasks would you most want an agent to handle autonomously — without a human in the loop?"
Separates automation candidates from tasks that require human judgment. Identifies the highest-value consumption units.
"Are there workflows today that a human does once, but that you wish could run continuously or at much higher volume?"
Surfaces scale multiplier opportunities — where agents create value not by replacing humans but by doing what humans can't do at scale.
"How do you currently measure whether those workflows are completing successfully?"
Identifies the customer's existing success metric — which often becomes the natural basis for an outcome-based billing unit.
Once workflows are mapped, quantify how much human effort the agents would displace. This builds the ROI numerator.
"How long does a person typically spend on [mapped workflow] today? And how often does it happen?"
Builds the time-per-task and frequency data needed to calculate hours displaced. Keep it conversational — don't ask them to produce a spreadsheet.
"If an agent could handle [workflow] autonomously, what would your team do with that time?"
Shifts the framing from cost reduction to capacity reallocation. More palatable to buyers who don't want to frame this as headcount reduction.
"Are there workflows you're currently not doing — or doing too slowly — because you don't have enough people?"
Identifies the capacity-constrained demand. Agents don't just displace existing work — they unlock work that wasn't getting done. This is often the larger ROI number.
Quantify the value in the customer's own terms. This becomes the ROI evidence that justifies the consumption model in the procurement conversation.
"If we could automate [workflow] fully — what would that be worth to your business in terms of time saved, revenue generated, or cost avoided?"
Ask them to put a number on it. Their number is always more credible in the internal approval process than your estimate.
"How does your business measure the success of this team or function today? What's the metric your leadership tracks?"
Connects your ROI to their existing KPIs. Makes the business case easier for the buyer to take up the chain.
"If you could point to one number — 6 months from now — that would tell you this investment paid off, what would it be?"
Identifies the success metric for the deal. This becomes the headline in your QBR, your renewal conversation, and your case study.
Help the customer forecast their expected consumption. This kills budget anxiety before it becomes an objection.
"Based on what you've described, you'd probably run around [X] agent tasks per month. Does that feel roughly right?"
Give them a number first — anchors the conversation and invites correction. Customers who feel heard on the estimate are much more comfortable with variable billing.
"Is there a budget owner who'd need to approve a variable cost line? What would they need to see to get comfortable?"
Surfaces the finance stakeholder before they become a deal blocker. Identifies what controls or reporting they'll need.
"Would it help to run through a projection of your expected spend at current usage — and what a growth scenario might look like?"
Opens the door to the commitment calculator conversation. Customers who see a bounded forecast close faster.

The three buyer personas and how each one buys
Chapter 03
The three personas — and how each one buys
Consumption pricing is a multi-stakeholder sale. The CPO cares about roadmap alignment. The CFO cares about budget predictability. The CRO cares about revenue impact. Each needs a different conversation.
The CPO — Product champion, roadmap owner
Primary concern: Does this model support our AI product strategy?
+
What they care about
The CPO wants to know that the pricing model supports — not constrains — the product roadmap. They're asking: will customers adopt the new agentic features if they're priced on consumption? Will a usage-based model slow down experimentation? Will customers feel penalised for trying new workflows?
How to position
Frame the consumption model as the commercial enabler of the product vision. Without it, AI features are undifferentiated add-ons in the base licence. With it, every new agent workflow becomes a revenue event. The product and the commercial model scale together.
Talk track
"Right now, every AI feature you ship goes into the base licence with no commercial signal. You can't tell which workflows are driving value and which aren't. With consumption pricing, usage data becomes your product signal — the workflows customers run most are the ones worth investing in. The model makes your roadmap decisions easier, not harder."
The CFO — Budget owner, risk manager
Primary concern: Can I predict and control this cost?
+
What they care about
The CFO's objection isn't really about the model — it's about variance. They've seen cloud bills spiral and budget owners shrug. They need to know this is bounded, forecastable, and controllable. Show them the ceiling, not just the floor.
How to position
Lead with the minimum commitment — that's a fixed, predictable number they can put in their model. Then show them the spend cap in the admin console. Then show them the consumption forecast based on current usage trajectory. Make the variance visible and bounded before they ask.
Talk track
"Your minimum commitment of £[X] per year is the number that goes into your budget model. It doesn't change unless you choose to upgrade. Above that, you have a hard cap in the admin console — nothing charges above it without your approval. Based on your current usage trajectory, you're tracking to about £[Y] this year. Would it help to send you a spend forecast you can use in your planning?"
The CRO — Revenue owner, GTM lead
Primary concern: What does this do to our team's quota and motion?
+
What they care about
The CRO is thinking about their team. How does consumption pricing affect quota attainment? How does CS expansion get credited? Are reps going to resist selling a model they don't understand? This is an internal stakeholder conversation as much as a product one.
How to position
Show them the expansion upside. Under a seat model, expansion is capped by headcount. Under a hybrid model, expansion is continuous — every new agent workflow is a tier upgrade conversation. Their team's NRR ceiling just got much higher. That's a good story for quota.
Talk track
"Under your current seat model, your reps can only expand when a customer hires more people. That's outside your control. With a consumption layer, every new agent use case is an expansion trigger your CSMs can actively drive. You're not waiting for headcount growth — you're creating your own expansion motion. Your NRR ceiling is significantly higher than it is today."

Chapter 04
The CS motion: from renewal defence to expansion engine
Under a seat model, CS is primarily defensive — protect the renewal, prevent churn. Under a consumption model, CS becomes the expansion engine. Agentic health replaces licence utilisation as the primary health signal.
The shift is structural. CS teams trained on "are seats being used?" need to move to "are agents creating value?" Those are different questions, and they require different data, different conversations, and different playbooks.

The QBR agenda for consumption models
1
Open with consumption data, not NPS
Show the customer their consumption trend — tasks completed, workflows run, outcomes delivered — before any relationship metrics. Lead with the value they've received.
2
Connect consumption to business outcomes
Translate the usage data into their language. "You ran 3,400 agent tasks last quarter — that's equivalent to approximately X hours of work your team didn't have to do." Ask them to validate or correct your estimate.
3
Identify the next use case
Always leave a QBR with a new agent workflow identified. This is your expansion pipeline — every new use case is a tier upgrade conversation 90 days later.
4
Review consumption against allowance
If they're above 70% of their allowance consistently, open the tier upgrade conversation now — before they hit overage and feel surprised. Proactive beats reactive every time.

Chapter 05
Comp design and the 30-day enablement plan
Commission plans optimised purely for ACV will produce the wrong commercial behaviours in a consumption model. If your reps are paid only on the commitment floor, they have no incentive to drive consumption growth — which is where the long-term revenue lives.
Comp design principles for hybrid models
Sales (AE)
Split commission: floor + consumption
70% of commission on the minimum commitment (ACV equivalent). 30% on a consumption accelerator — paid quarterly based on actual usage above the floor. Aligns AE incentive with long-term consumption growth.
CS (CSM)
NRR-based with consumption bonus
Base commission on NRR target. Bonus pool paid when accounts consistently hit 80%+ of consumption allowance — triggering upgrade conversations. Rewards health management, not just renewal defence.
RevOps
Consumption forecast accuracy
Add a consumption forecast metric to RevOps KPIs. Teams that can accurately forecast consumption growth 90 days out give finance the confidence to plan — and give leadership the visibility to invest.
Leadership
Consumption as % of total ARR
The board-level metric: consumption revenue as a percentage of total ARR, growing toward a defined target (20–35% within 18 months of model launch).

The enablement clock starts at launch
Every week your GTM team operates under the new pricing model without proper enablement is a week of consumption revenue you won't recover. Discovery conversations revert to seat qualification. CS health metrics stay on licence utilisation. The model exists on the pricing page but not in the commercial motion. Enablement is not a follow-up activity — it's a launch requirement.
