Chapter 01
The infrastructure gap that kills consumption pricing before it launches
Most SaaS companies design a compelling consumption model — then discover their infrastructure can't support it. Billing systems built for fixed subscriptions. No real-time telemetry. CPQ with no consumption SKUs. The commercial model is ready. The operational stack is not.
Consumption pricing is operationally demanding in ways that seat pricing is not. Every billable action needs to be captured, attributed, aggregated, and invoiced — automatically, accurately, and in real time. Manual processes between metering and billing introduce errors at the worst possible moment: when a customer questions their invoice.

"A consumption pricing model is only as good as the infrastructure underneath it. If you can't meter it reliably, you can't bill it credibly."

FR Advisory
The six infrastructure layers
📡
Layer 1 — Event telemetry
Every agent action, task completion, or billable event is captured as a structured event with timestamp, customer ID, and action type. This is the raw data layer — without it, nothing else works.
Key question: Can you log every billable event in real time?
🔢
Layer 2 — Aggregation & metering
Raw events are aggregated into consumption totals by customer, time period, and usage type. This layer answers the question: "how much has this customer used this month?"
Key question: Can you aggregate usage without manual reconciliation?
💳
Layer 3 — Billing engine
Consumption totals flow into a billing engine that calculates charges, applies tier rates, handles overages, and generates invoices. Must support variable charges, credits, and commit drawdown.
Key question: Does your billing platform handle variable consumption?
🛒
Layer 4 — CPQ & contracts
Sales teams need consumption SKUs they can quote, commit structures they can contract, and pricing calculators that show customers their expected spend. Without this layer, the sales team can't sell the model.
Key question: Do consumption SKUs exist in your CPQ?
📊
Layer 5 — Customer visibility
Customers need real-time dashboards showing their consumption, projected spend, and remaining allowance. Spend caps and alerts are not optional extras — they are the trust infrastructure that makes consumption pricing acceptable to enterprise buyers.
Key question: Can customers see their usage in real time?
📈
Layer 6 — Finance reporting
Revenue must be reported separately: base platform fees vs consumption revenue. Blended reporting hides the growth lever and makes investor conversations significantly harder.
Key question: Can finance separate base from consumption revenue?

Building reliable event telemetry
Chapter 02
Telemetry: the foundation everything else depends on
Without reliable event telemetry, consumption pricing is a commercial promise built on operational guesswork. Getting the data layer right is the most important infrastructure investment you'll make.
Most SaaS products already generate usage data — server logs, API call records, workflow completion events. The problem is that this data was never designed with billing in mind. It's inconsistent, incomplete, and not connected to customer identity in a way that can be used for invoicing.
What a billable event must contain
Required
Customer identifier
A persistent, unambiguous ID that maps the event to a specific billing account — not a user ID or session ID that changes.
Required
Event timestamp
UTC timestamp with millisecond precision. Critical for time-period aggregation and dispute resolution.
Required
Action type
A defined taxonomy of billable action types. Must map directly to the customer-facing definition of what counts as billable.
Required
Completion status
Whether the action completed successfully. Partial or failed actions must be handled by your billing rules — not ignored.
Recommended
Workflow context
The workflow or use case that triggered the event. Enables per-workflow reporting and dispute handling.
Recommended
Resource consumed
Compute, tokens, or processing time consumed. Useful for cost-to-serve analysis even if not exposed to customers.
The three telemetry failure modes
Failure modeWhat happensThe fix
Event loss
Events dropped under load		Under-billing. Customers pay less than they owe. Revenue leakage compounds over time.Use a durable event queue (Kafka, SQS). Never log direct to DB in the critical path.
Double counting
Retries logged as new events		Over-billing. Invoice disputes. Customer trust destroyed in one bad month.Idempotency keys on every event. Deduplication layer before aggregation.
Attribution gaps
Events not linked to billing account		Unattributed consumption. Revenue recognised but no customer to bill.Customer ID injected at the SDK/API layer — never resolved post-hoc.
The audit log is non-negotiable
Every billable event must be immutably logged with a customer-accessible audit trail. When a customer disputes a charge — and they will — your ability to show them the exact events that generated the invoice is the difference between a 10-minute resolution and a three-week escalation. Build the audit log before you bill the first consumption invoice.

CPQ, contracts, and finance reporting
Chapter 04
CPQ, contracts, and finance reporting
Telemetry and billing solve the back-end. CPQ and finance reporting solve the front-end — the commercial infrastructure your sales team uses to sell and your finance team uses to report.
CPQ requirements
Sales teams can only sell what they can quote. If consumption SKUs don't exist in your CPQ, the model doesn't get sold — regardless of how well the commercial team understands it. CPQ for consumption pricing needs three specific capabilities:
Must have
Consumption SKUs
Defined SKUs for each tier's consumption allowance plus the overage rate. Not custom line items — actual product codes that feed into contract terms automatically.
Must have
Commitment calculator
A tool reps can use in the deal to show customers their projected annual spend based on current and expected usage. Kills the "I can't forecast this" objection in the room.
Important
Tier upgrade logic
Automated triggers when a customer's usage pattern suggests an upgrade conversation is warranted — typically at 80% consistent utilisation of their current allowance.
Finance reporting requirements
Blended revenue reporting — platform fees and consumption revenue combined — hides the growth story and complicates investor conversations. Finance needs to report separately on:
1
Base ARR (platform commitments)
The recurring, predictable component — minimum commitments across all active customers. This is your floor ARR and the number that supports your valuation multiple.
2
Consumption revenue (above commitment)
Variable revenue from usage above the included allowance. Track as a separate line with month-over-month growth rate — this is your expansion vector story.
3
Consumption as % of total revenue
The ratio that shows the model is working. As consumption revenue grows as a share of total, it validates that the hybrid model is capturing agentic value. Target: 20–35% within 18 months of launch.
4
GRR and NRR by pricing model cohort
Track retention and expansion separately for hybrid-model customers vs legacy seat customers. This is your proof that the transition is commercially justified.
The reporting gap is a board risk
Boards and investors increasingly understand hybrid models — but they need clean data to evaluate them. If your consumption revenue isn't separated in your reporting, the growth story gets buried in blended numbers. Build the reporting separation before your next board pack, not after the questions start.

Your 45-day infrastructure action plan
Chapter 05
Your 45-day infrastructure action plan
Infrastructure work is sequenced differently from commercial work. The telemetry layer must come first — everything else depends on it. Don't start billing system work until event capture is reliable.
W1–2
Telemetry audit
Audit existing event logs. Identify gaps in customer attribution, event completeness, and real-time capability. Define the billable event schema.
W2–3
Event pipeline build
Implement durable event queue, idempotency keys, and deduplication. Build the immutable audit log. Test under load before billing goes live.
W3–4
Billing platform config
Configure consumption SKUs, credit models, overage rates, and spend cap logic in your billing platform. Run a parallel billing test against real usage data.
W4–6
CPQ, reporting & customer dashboard
Add consumption SKUs to CPQ. Build separated revenue reporting. Launch customer-facing usage dashboard with real-time consumption and alert thresholds.
